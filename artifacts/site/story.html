Our story

It began with one little girl.

Brian BetheaCo‑founder, Glucose Guardian

My journey with Type 1 diabetes began on a night my wife and I will never forget.

Our daughter was just 11 months old when we began noticing changes we couldn’t explain. She was drinking unusually large amounts of water, becoming increasingly irritable, and having trouble sleeping. At first, my wife and I thought she might be teething or simply recovering from a fever. We had no idea these could be warning signs of something much more serious.

One night, her discomfort became impossible to ignore. She couldn’t settle down to sleep, and her breathing became noticeably heavier. My wife and I knew something wasn’t right, so we decided to take her to the hospital.

After what felt like an endless series of examinations and tests, the medical team finally returned with an answer we never expected.

Our daughter’s blood sugar was over 700. She needed insulin immediately.

In that moment, everything changed.

What we thought might be a temporary illness turned into a life‑changing diagnosis of Type 1 diabetes. My wife and I were overwhelmed with emotions, questions, and fears about what the future would look like for our little girl.

The months and years that followed became a journey of learning, adjusting, and navigating a new reality. From sleepless nights monitoring glucose levels to treating unexpected lows, managing insulin, and understanding how different foods affected her blood sugar, my wife and I learned that diabetes management required constant attention.

Over time, we became familiar with her patterns, her needs, and the little signs that something wasn’t quite right.

But when our daughter turned four and prepared to begin school in August 2026, we faced an entirely different challenge.

For the first time, we would have to place her diabetes care in someone else’s hands.

It wasn’t about a lack of trust in her teachers or caregivers. It was about the uncertainty of not knowing what was happening throughout her day.

Was she eating her lunch? Had she received her insulin? What if her glucose levels suddenly dropped or climbed? Would someone recognize when she wasn’t feeling like herself?

My wife and I wanted our daughter to experience the joy and independence of childhood without diabetes defining every moment. But we also wanted to remain connected to her care, even when we couldn’t physically be there.

During a conversation with my friend and now business partner, Brett, I shared some of the concerns surrounding my daughter’s upcoming transition to school. Brett understood the challenges of diabetes from a different perspective, as his father also lives with Type 1 diabetes.

What began as a simple conversation turned into an idea.

What if we could create something that helped people living with diabetes, their families, and their caregivers stay more connected and informed?

That conversation became the beginning of Glucose Guardian.

As Brett and I began developing the platform, I continued thinking about the challenges my wife and I had experienced. What information would have helped us? What would give us greater peace of mind? How could we make communication between parents, caregivers, and schools easier?

Those questions helped shape the foundation of Glucose Guardian.

Although the inspiration came from my daughter’s experience with Type 1 diabetes, Brett and I recognized an opportunity to serve a much broader community.

Today, as Co‑Founders, Brett and I are building Glucose Guardian to support individuals living with Type 1 and Type 2 diabetes, along with the families and caregivers who support them.

For me, Glucose Guardian is more than an app. It’s a personal mission born from the challenges my wife and I have faced, the lessons we’ve learned, and the desire to make a meaningful difference in the lives of others.

My journey began with one little girl. My mission is to help make diabetes management more connected, accessible, and less overwhelming for everyone.

Our mission

Glucose Guardian was born from personal experiences with diabetes and a shared belief that managing it shouldn’t mean navigating it alone. What began as a desire to protect one little girl has grown into a mission to empower individuals living with Type 1 and Type 2 diabetes, their families, and their caregivers through better connection, communication, and peace of mind.
